We investigate how intent representation and pedestrian attention shape legible robot navigation in constrained, dynamic hallways. Two controlled user studies with a Hello Robot Stretch 2 show that expressing an immediate passing-side choice, particularly when adapted to human motion, supports smoother coordination than expressing a final destination. These coordination benefits persist even when pedestrians are distracted.

This journal paper extends our [HRI 2026 late-breaking report](/publication/2026-03-16-Rethinking-Legibility-in-Social-Robot-Navigation).
